# Kings Property History Report  

## Origin Date and Type  
The property began as a mulberry plantation in James I’s early 17th‑century garden, later acquired by John Sheffield who demolished it to build the original Buckingham House at £7,000【[1]】. The building was originally constructed in 1703 as Buckingham House and is situated within Westminster, London【[1]】.  

## Ownership Timeline  
The house had many owners and tenants until, in 1698, it was let to the man who gave it its name – John Sheffield, later the Duke of Buckingham【[2]】. By 1828 Nash spent £496,169 on changes to the building, far above budget【[2]】. George III bought Buckingham House for Queen Charlotte in 1761 for £21,000, after which it became “Queen’s House” (1762‑1776)【[2]】. John Nash expanded it into an imposing U‑shaped palace costing £496,169 over budget【[3]】; Edward Blore completed the State Rooms (1833‑34) using Carlton House furnishings【[3]】. Queen Victoria took up residence in 1837, making it the official London monarchial home, and the property has remained the central venue for the monarchy ever since, with King Charles III having moved to Clarence House in 2022 but the palace continuing to serve as a state‑visit centre【[4]】.  

## Notable Historical Events  
- A major fire occurred in 2007 that prompted security upgrades【[1]】.  
- Bombing raids destroyed the private chapel in September 1940, contributing to a public gallery【[3]】.  
- Prince William stopped traffic outside Buckingham Palace for a game of cricket (October 2026)【[1]】.  
- King Charles III acknowledges “darkest days” of Britain’s colonial rule during his Caribbean tour【[1]】.  

## Architectural Significance  
John Nash expanded the palace into an imposing U‑shaped structure, costing £496,169 over budget【[3]】; Edward Blore completed the State Rooms (1833‑34) using Carlton House furnishings【[3]】. Queen Victoria added a wing and central balcony in 1845‑46 for troops, inspired by Prince Albert’s suggestion【[3]】. King Edward VII redecorated interiors from 1901‑02【[3]】. The palace spans roughly 350 ft across, 400 ft deep and reaches about 80 ft high, comprising 19 meeting/staterooms, 52 bedrooms, 92 offices and 78 bathrooms; the largest space is the Ballroom【[4]】.  

## Legal and Property Status  
The Crown Estate is a unique UK asset owned by the Monarch “in right of the Crown”; its profits go to the Treasury, which then decides the annual payment known as the Sovereign Grant【[3]】. Buckingham Palace is owned by the royal family; the property is managed by the Royal Collection Trust just as every property or piece of art is managed【[2]】.  

## Current Location  
The structure is located in Westminster, south of London, England at 10 Westminster Lane, London, UK【[1]】.
